Risk management

Risk management essentials

Position sizing, stop-loss and take-profit orders, and simple rules that help protect your capital when markets move against you.

Varexa Markets Education 5 Nov 2026 7 min readAll levels

Every trader has losing trades. Risk management is about making sure that no single loss, or run of losses, does serious damage to your account. These are the essentials.

Decide your risk per trade

Many traders limit the amount they could lose on one trade to a small percentage of their account, often between 1% and 2%. On a 5,000 account, a 1% limit means a maximum loss of 50 on any trade. This keeps you in the market through a losing streak.

Use stop-loss orders

A stop-loss order closes your position automatically if price reaches a level you choose. Place it where your trade idea is proven wrong, not at a random distance. In fast or gapping markets, a standard stop can be filled at a worse price than requested.

Size the position from the stop

Once you know your maximum loss and the distance to your stop, you can work out your position size:

Position size = Amount at risk ÷ (Stop distance in pips × Pip value per lot)

For example, risking 50 with a 25-pip stop on a pair where one standard lot is worth 10 per pip gives 50 ÷ (25 × 10) = 0.2 lots.

Plan the reward

A take-profit order closes the trade at a target. Comparing the distance to your target with the distance to your stop gives the risk-to-reward ratio. A trade risking 25 pips to make 50 has a ratio of 1:2.

Respect leverage

Using the maximum available leverage is rarely necessary. Lower effective leverage gives your trades more room and reduces the risk of a margin call.

Key takeaways

  • Set a maximum risk per trade before you enter.
  • Place stops where the trade idea fails, then size the position to match.
  • Know your risk-to-reward ratio before you click buy or sell.
