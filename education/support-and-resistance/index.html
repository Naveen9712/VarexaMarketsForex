Support and resistance: finding key price levels

Why prices often pause or reverse at the same levels, how to draw support and resistance on your charts, and how traders use them.

Varexa Markets Education 22 Oct 2026 6 min readIntermediate

Support and resistance are price areas where buying or selling has repeatedly been strong enough to stop a move. They are among the most widely used tools in technical analysis.

What they are

Support is a level where falling prices have tended to stop because buyers step in. Resistance is a level where rising prices have tended to stall because sellers take control.

How to draw them

  • Start on a higher timeframe, such as the daily chart.
  • Look for areas where price has turned at least twice.
  • Draw zones rather than exact lines, because price rarely turns at the same tick.
  • Round numbers, such as 1.1000 or 4,000, often act as levels too.

When levels switch roles

When price breaks clearly through resistance, that level often becomes support on a later retest, and broken support often becomes resistance. Traders call this a role reversal.

How traders use them

Some traders buy near support with a stop just below it, or sell near resistance with a stop just above it. Others wait for a confirmed break and trade in the direction of the breakout. Either way, the level gives a logical place for a stop loss.

Key takeaways

  • Support and resistance are zones, not exact prices.
  • The more times a level holds, the more traders notice it.
  • Levels can break. Always use a stop loss.
