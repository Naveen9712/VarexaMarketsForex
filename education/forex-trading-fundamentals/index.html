Forex trading fundamentals: pips, lots and leverage

The building blocks of every forex trade explained in plain language: currency pairs, bid and ask prices, pips, lot sizes and how leverage works.

Varexa Markets Education 12 Nov 2026 6 min readBeginner

Forex is the market for exchanging one currency for another. Every price you see compares two currencies, and every trade is a decision about whether the first will strengthen or weaken against the second. Before placing a trade, it helps to understand five terms that appear on every quote.

Currency pairs

Currencies are quoted in pairs such as EUR/USD. The first currency is the base and the second is the quote currency. A price of 1.0862 means one euro costs 1.0862 US dollars. If you buy EUR/USD, you profit if the euro rises against the dollar; if you sell, you profit if it falls.

Bid, ask and spread

Every quote has two prices. The bid is the price you can sell at, and the ask is the price you can buy at. The gap between them is the spread, which is one of the main costs of trading.

Pips

A pip is the standard unit for measuring price moves. For most pairs it is the fourth decimal place (0.0001). For pairs quoted against the Japanese yen, it is the second decimal place (0.01). Many platforms show a fifth digit, called a fractional pip or pipette.

Lot sizes

Trade size is measured in lots. A standard lot is 100,000 units of the base currency, a mini lot is 10,000 and a micro lot is 1,000. Your lot size decides how much each pip is worth: on EUR/USD, one pip on a standard lot is worth about 10 US dollars, and on a micro lot about 10 cents.

Leverage and margin

Leverage lets you control a larger position with a smaller deposit, called margin. With 1:30 leverage, for example, a position worth 30,000 needs about 1,000 of margin. Leverage magnifies losses as well as gains, so a small move against you can use up your margin quickly.

Key takeaways

  • A currency pair shows how much of the quote currency buys one unit of the base currency.
  • The spread is the difference between the buy and sell price.
  • Lot size decides how much each pip is worth to you.
  • Leverage increases both potential gains and potential losses.
