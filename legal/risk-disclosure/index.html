How leverage works

Margin is the amount required to open or maintain a position. Your exposure can exceed that margin. For illustration, an exposure of USD 10,000 with USD 500 in initial margin is equivalent to 20:1 leverage. A 1% adverse price move produces a USD 100 loss before costs, equal to 20% of that initial margin. Actual margin, prices and fees depend on the instrument and account. Market movements and forced closure

Volatility can widen spreads, reduce available liquidity and trigger rapid losses. Prices may gap when markets reopen or after major news. Positions may be closed automatically if your account reaches its stop-out level. Market conditions can affect the price and timing of that closure. A stop-loss is a trigger rather than a guaranteed execution price unless a separately disclosed guaranteed order applies.

Product and counterparty risk

A CFD gives you economic exposure to an underlying price without ownership of the asset. Your rights are against the counterparty named in your agreement. The ability to recover money can depend on the provider, the fund-holding arrangement and applicable insolvency rules. Read the Client Agreement for the actual arrangement and any applicable protection.

Costs and currency risk

Spreads, commission, overnight financing and conversion charges can reduce gains or increase losses. Exchange-rate changes can affect profit and loss in your account currency. Check the contract specifications and cost schedule before placing a trade.

Technology and third-party tools

Connection outages, platform incidents or delayed data can affect order submission and visibility. Automated tools can send orders or increase exposure faster than you can intervene. Historical results and simulated trading do not establish future performance. If you use any separate copy trading or managed service, read its specific risk disclosure.
